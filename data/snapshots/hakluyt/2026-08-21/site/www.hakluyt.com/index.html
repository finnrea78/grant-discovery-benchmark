Hakluyt Society for voyages of discovery, history of maritime exploration, history of navigation, historical travel accounts

Since its foundation in 1846, the Hakluyt Society has published scholarly editions of primary records of voyages and travels. Hakluyt Society books are often real-life adventure stories in the field of exploration, navigation, voyages of discovery and historical travel accounts.

These volumes are scholarly page turners, fully annotated with fascinating details of tall ships, remote lands, newly sighted plant and animal species, and peoples rarely visited at the time, often the first descriptions of these distant cultures. With over 380 volumes published, this remains our principal activity, while we now extend our work to an online journal, conferences, lectures and the award of grants and prizes. The volumes, which are distributed to current members, are illustrated with maps and plates and are widely prized for their standards of scholarship and book production. For members they are incredibly good value for money, compared with commercial prices.

The Society welcomes proposals from potential editors of texts for publication. The Society is proud of the diversity of its publications which include different types of travel accounts and cover almost all parts of the world. As well as records of voyages, we especially seek travel accounts which have been under-represented in our publications, including texts written by women and by non-Europeans, and texts which document the experiences of indigenous peoples. The text has to be interesting to read and intrinsically important, documenting an achievement in the description and understanding of the world and its peoples or an historically, politically, economically or culturally significant encounter with different peoples.

The Hakluyt Society is named after Richard Hakluyt (1552-1616), collector and editor of narratives of voyages and travels and other documents relating to English interests overseas, his most celebrated work being The Principal Navigations, Voyages and Discoveries of the English Nation. His name was almost certainly pronounced hak’loowt, the family having come from the forest of Clwyd in the historic county of Radnor.

“The Hakluyt Society volumes are the Gold Standard for historical eyewitness accounts of exploration and discovery, annotated in detail by first rank scholars and beautifully bound.” Dr. Jack Benson, University of Zurich.

Markham translated and got Hakluyt to publish a bonanza (for me) of texts about the Incas, which were invaluable in my research.” John Henry Hemming CMG, historian and explorer.

“For more than 150 years, the Hakluyt Society’s many publications, the texts carefully edited according to modern principles and accompanied by informative maps, charts and illustrations, have constituted a matchless resource for those interested in Europe’s explorations of the outer world.” Prof. Alan Frost, FRHistS, FAHA, La Trobe University.

“I stumbled on the Society’s books in my teens. I’m still entranced with the wonder I felt then at worlds disclosed through the minds of discoverers to whom everything was strange and transforming. Each new volume enriches an eagle-eyed vision of Earth – sweeping in scope, vivid in detail.” Felipe Fernández-Armesto, University of Notre Dame.

Membership of the Hakluyt Society is open to all and is strongly recommended to everybody interested in the history of exploration and travel, exploratory voyages, geographical discovery and worldwide cultural encounter.

On payment of an annual subscription, members receive all volumes issued by the Society (other than those of the Extra Series) for the year of membership. These historically significant texts and translations of accounts of exploration, navigation and travel, often appearing in print for the first time, are fully annotated, well illustrated with maps and plates, and conform to the highest standards of scholarship. As such they often represent the ‘last word’ on the material they embrace, and are widely valued by historians and geographers throughout the world. Normally, two volumes are published each year, representing extraordinary value for money. These books are beautifully bound and printed, and stamped in gold with the familiar ‘Victoria’ logo of the Society, and rapidly become treasured collectors’ items.

Upcoming 2026 Hakluyt Society Volume

A Booke of the Travaile and Lief of Me Thomas Hoby, with Diverse Thinges Woorth the Notinge, 1547-1564, in modern form as A Book of the Travels and Life of Me, Thomas Hoby.

This volume should be going to the publisher in June 2026 and will be sent out to members in the second half of the year. It is edited by Kenneth Bartlett of the University of Toronto.

Thomas Hoby (born 1530, in Leominster, Herefordshire and died 13 July, 1566, in Paris) was educated at Cambridge, Strasbourg and Padua, and traveled extensively on the European continent. Given court employments in England under King Edward VI, he went into exile during the reign of Mary I. While in exile he translated Il Cortegioano by Castlgione, which he published as The Courtyer of Count Baldesser Castilio in 1561. The influence of Hoby’s translation in England was enormous, not only on the social pattern of life at court but on such writers as Shakespeare and Sir Philip Sidney. Hoby also translated a Latin work on the Church of England. Hoby’s tour of Italy, which included visits to Calabria and Sicily and which he documented in the volume we are publishing, is the most extensive known to have been undertaken by an Englishman in the 16th century. In this and other respects, it may be regarded as a pioneering Grand Tour. Hoby was knighted by Elizabeth I in 1566 and sent to Paris as English ambassador. He died that same year.

Hakluyt Society Essay Competition 2026

The Hakluyt Society is delighted to announce this year’s results.

This year’s winner of the first prize of £1250 is Dr Agnieszka Anna Ficek, for her essay ‘‘The Spy and the Strawberry: Amédée-François Frézier’s Botanical Discoveries in Colonial Peru’.

Second prize of £250 was awarded to Dr Louise McCarthy, for ‘‘Corporate Business and Scientific Rhetoric in Early Modern English Cartography (1580s-1620s)’

Many congratulations to Agnieszka and Louise, who have been invited to receive their awards at the Society’s AGM on 24 June 2026. Details of how to enter the Hakluyt Society Essay Prize Competition 2027 will be announced later in the year.
