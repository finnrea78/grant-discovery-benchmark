Unlocking Futures Fund

Our Unlocking Futures Fund is open to postdoctoral researchers who require an additional support to help them achieve their full potential.

The next application round is now open!

Apply now

Applications will close on Monday 28 September 2026 at 23:59 BST.

 

Read Professor Hisako Ikeda-Wolstencroft Story

The Unlocking Futures Fund has been started in memory of Professor Hisako Ikeda-Wolstencroft, a valued member of the Society and a world leader in the field of vision research. In her memory, Professor Ikeda-Wolstencroft’s family made the founding donation to the Society’s Unlocking Futures Fund with the aim of unlocking the potential of a future leader of physiology.

Help us unlock the potential of a future leader of the discipline by donating to the fund. Your donation will help sustain this avenue of support for members to access when they need it most. We can help this incredible community overcome barriers and contribute to advancements in physiology.

Donate now

Terms and conditions
  • Funds should be used within 12 months of the date of receipt, supporting the activity outlined in the application.
  • Applicants should demonstrate how this fund would enhance their commitment to physiology and benefit the wider community of physiologists.
  • Applications must be received by the deadlines laid out above. Applications not received in this timeframe will not be considered.
  • Members will normally only be awarded this funding once during their period of membership.
  • You cannot apply for the same activity if a previous application has been rejected within that year.
  • Successful applicants must acknowledge the TPS funding in all their research activities which relate to the award. For example, the TPS logo must be presented in all conference presentations (available on request) and the TPS must be acknowledged in all publications.
  • All awardees will be required to submit a report following the Award. If such a report is not submitted the holder will be deemed ineligible for any other funding from The Physiological Society. Any funds not used at the end of the Award period must be returned to The Society.
  • Funds cannot be used to pay for staff salaries but may be used for consumables, registration for Higher Education Academy Fellowship or other sundries that can be fully justified.
  • In the event of a submission being unsuccessful, we are unable to give feedback.
  • The final decision of the awarding panel is not subject to appeal.
  • This award will not be reimbursed to any members with an outstanding balance or direct debit arrears on their record. All arrears must be cleared for claims to be paid.

Past projects

View the past recipients we have supported through the Unlocking Futures Fund

2023 –

  • Dr Tessa Flood, University of West London, UK – to establish a method to accurately assess hormonal profiles (endogenous and exogenous) in women taking hormonal contraceptives using an internal standard method for LC-MS/MS analysis.
  • Dr Yusuke Nishimura, Liverpool John Moores University, UK – to help bridge the gap between physiology and bioinformatics to tackle complex mechanisms of skeletal muscle ageing by using an advanced proteomic technique.

2024 –

  • Dr Amanda MacCannell, University of Leeds, UK – to support obtaining a Global Talent Visa.
  • Dr Katie Partridge, University of Exeter, UK – for a pilot project focused on developing a stem cell-derived pancreas co-culture system to model pancreatic physiology and pathophysiology.
  • Dr Risha Amarsi, Kings College London, UK – to conduct a pilot study of SOCS2 protein expression in the pregnant mouse brain.
  • Dr Rita Civil, University of Birmingham, UK – for laboratory consumables to conduct pilot experiments.

2025 –

  • Dr Lindsay Robinson, University of Nottingam, UK – to optimise and extend the physiological measurements of the CoolBAT study.
  • Dr Mhairi Paul, The University of Edinburgh, UK – to purchase the specialist surgical and HEC equipment necessary to conduct our first in vivo pilot study in mice,  laying the foundation for critical advancements in metabolic physiology.
  • Dr Rebecca Gilchrist, University of Glasgow, UK – to attend a Cell Physiology Workshop.
  • Dr Thamali Ayagama, University of Oxford, UK – to investigate Neuropeptide-Y (NPY) pathophysiology in the development of heart failure, using human and rat tissue of normal and CHF heart.
  • Dr Elisa Nédélec, Northumbria University, UK – to attend a four-week research placement at the University of Brighton.
  • Dr Thomas Inn, Nottingham Trent University, UK – to conduct research stemming from a large scale funded study investigating biomarkers for concussion recovery prediction (mTBI Predict).
