Grants & Funding Opportunities

Event Support Grants

Open for applications

Support for organisations aiming to disseminate research through events and programming

Our Event Support Grants are intended to support the dissemination of research on British art through live, online and hybrid events put on by an organisation and intended for a variety of audiences.

The grants are for any amount up to £3,000.

Event Support Grants are available to any organisation, institution or nonprofit and collaborative applications are welcomed.

Applications for the Autumn 2026 round of funding are now open, applications close on Monday 14 September. Apply here.

Funding

Organisations can claim for any amount up to £3,000 and all applicants will need to provide a detailed budget of how the funding will be spent. We expect events to take place within the year of the award being made and, as such, the budget should be as accurate as possible.

Costs that can be claimed for include:

Venue

  • hiring of space or venue
  • catering costs
  • equipment hire

Access

  • wheelchair ramps
  • induction loop
  • sign language interpreter fees
  • subsidised/free places for students or those facing financial barriers

Speakers

  • speaker fees/honorariums (we expect these costs to be in line with recommended rates)
  • travel and accommodation. There are no budget limitations regarding these costs however we would expect them to be reasonable depending on the options available. We encourage applicants to consider the environmental impact of travel options when planning the event.

Staff

  • organiser fees (we expect these costs to be in line with recommended rates)
  • support staff

Online Events

  • virtual event hosting (eg. Zoom subscription, live captioning etc.)

Other Costs

This list is not exhaustive however if you would like to claim for costs not listed above please do speak to our Grants Manager before making your application.

Outcomes

The support from this grant should result in an event, public programming or similar form of research dissemination.

Eligibility

Applicant
Our Event Support Grants are available to any to any organisations, institutions, nonprofits, or other entities which hold a dedicated business account. Collaborative applications between two or more organisations are welcomed however one organisation will need to be named as the lead administrator. Applications from internationally based organisations are also eligible.

Event
All applicants must demonstrate how the grant will enhance their event on British art, visual culture and/or architecture. More information on our subject criteria is available on our Our Funding Focus webpage and we have also highlighted some of our previously funded projects here.

Supported event types include lectures, conferences, workshops, gallery talks, symposia and seminars. Events such as film screenings or artistic performances may qualify if accompanied by research-based talks or presentations. The event should be standalone or a dedicated series, rather than part of the organisation’s regular programming.

Application Content

For Event Support Grants your application should include:

  • a detailed and thorough outline of the event with details on how it intends to contribute to the wider British art field; ideally the event should be in the final phases of planning, with speaker names, papers and venue details provided as part of the application
  • a comprehensive budget with the items due to be supported by the grant clearly indicated
  • all applications must clearly state how the event intends to showcase or share academic research

References are not required for Event Support Grant applications.

The number of good applications is likely to exceed the funding available, so the Advisory Council are advised to use some or all of the following criteria to help them in making decisions on how best to allocate funding:

  • academic rigour
  • originality and distinctness in the context of current scholarship
  • contribution to scholarship in the longer term
  • timeliness, where a project is connected with a particular moment or opportunity
  • where a grant would support work in a geographical or cultural context which has not hitherto been supported by PMC
  • need, where the research would be unlikely to go ahead without this support
  • impact and inclusion, where the research would include voices or reach a broader range of people than have hitherto been represented in the field of British art studies
  • feasibility and quality of application; the information provided is thorough, clear and demonstrates that the project can be undertaken in the time and manner proposed
  • value for money and environmental impact; the research has a realistic and appropriate budget and environmental impacts have been given consideration (eg. alternative forms of transport

You should read our Frequently Asked Questions and our Our Funding Focus webpages carefully to ensure eligibility and to make your application as strong as possible.

The application does not require any references.

Application Process

  1. Submit Your Application
    All applications must be filled our via grants.paul-mellon-centre.ac.uk. Once registered you will be able to select the relevant opportunity and fill out the application form, this will include providing the details of your referee.
  2. Assessment
    We undertake a two-round assessment process. In the first round all applications are read and assessed by a Sub-Committee of our Advisory Council members. A percentage of the top scoring applications will then proceed to the second and final round of assessment, involving all of our Advisory Council members, which will conclude in a decision meeting where the grants are awarded.
  3. Final Decision
    We will keep you updated about the status of your application throughout the assessment process and will inform all applicants of the final decisions by the date given at the time of application.

Restrictions

  • We cannot fund any activity that will have taken place before the date that grant decisions are made therefore all events that receive this grant will need to be forthcoming at the time of application and should be planned to take place after the date in which decisions are communicated to applicants, for our Autumn round this will be late November and for our Spring round this will be early April.
  • We cannot support events solely aimed at children or a family orientated audience.

Access

Accessible Formats: We can provide large text or clear text formats of our application forms and guidance upon request. If you have any other access requirements regarding our website or experience any issues using our online grants system then please contact us.

Access Funding: We are committed to making our opportunities accessible to everyone. To support this, we have set aside funding to help individuals who may face additional barriers and as such incur extra costs whilst undertaking their research projects. If your application is successful, we will be happy to discuss how this funding can support your additional needs.

Recipient Requirements

  • If successful then the recipient will need to adhere to our Terms & Conditions. This includes providing a thorough final report within 3 months of completing the award term.
  • The organisation should acknowledge the support of the Paul Mellon Centre through any materials associated with the event, full guidelines for acknowledgment and copies of the logo will be provided.
