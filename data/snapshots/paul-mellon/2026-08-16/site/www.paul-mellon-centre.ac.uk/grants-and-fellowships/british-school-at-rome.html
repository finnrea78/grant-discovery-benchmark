British School at Rome

The British School at Rome, ,

In 2026, the British School at Rome (BSR) celebrates its 125th anniversary. Since its foundation in 1901, the BSR has played a pivotal role in shaping international scholarship and artistic practice, serving as a vital bridge between Rome — one of the world’s great centres of cultural heritage — and creative and academic communities across Britain, the Commonwealth and beyond.

For one hundred and twenty-five years, the BSR has nurtured generations of artists and researchers whose work has had lasting impact in universities, museums, galleries and cultural institutions worldwide. Its alumni include many of the most distinguished and influential figures in twentieth- and twenty-first-century art and scholarship, from Thomas Monnington, Winifred Knights and John Skeaping to more recent artists such as Eddie Peake, Cornelia Parker and Mark Wallinger. Through its awards and fellowships, the BSR continues to support individuals whose work shapes public understanding of the ancient world, the histories of Italy and the Mediterranean, and contemporary visual culture.

Each year, the BSR offers outstanding researchers and artists the opportunity to join a dynamic residential community in Rome. At any one time, around thirty fellows are in residence, working across visual arts, architecture, archaeology, art history, history and all periods of Italian studies. The shared life of the School — communal dining, site visits, workshops, lectures and public events — fosters sustained interdisciplinary exchange and international collaboration, generating work that resonates far beyond Rome.

The BSR is also home to internationally significant research collections that support scholars from around the world. Its specialist library, archives and graphic collections are essential resources for the study of Italy and the Mediterranean across all periods. Among the most notable are:

The Photographic Collection
Comprising nearly one hundred thousand items — including calotypes, glass negatives, lantern slides, 35 mm film and postcards — the Photographic Collection preserves material dating from the 1850s to the late twentieth century. Its holdings encompass Greek and Roman antiquity as well as medieval and Renaissance Italian and European art and architecture, including material from the collections of Robert Macpherson, John Henry Parker, Peter Paul Mackey, Thomas Ashby, John Bryan Ward-Perkins and others. These collections continue to inform new research and international exhibitions.

Archive
The administrative archive documents the BSR’s activities since 1901, providing an unparalleled record of artistic and scholarly exchange between Britain and Italy. It includes material relating to awardees, organisational charters, Council papers, Directors’ correspondence, exhibitions and lectures, alongside important private archives donated to the BSR.

Library
The BSR Library is a specialist resource for the study of Italian art, archaeology, architecture and history, holding over 100,000 volumes and a wide range of journals, databases, and digital resources. It includes extensive holdings on ancient, medieval and modern Italy, supporting research in art history, archaeology, architecture, history, and related disciplines. The Library actively supports the work of BSR fellows and external researchers, providing both reference and lending services and access to rare and unique materials.
Explore the BSR Library

Rare Book and Special Collections
At the heart of the Rare Book Collection are over one thousand volumes acquired by Thomas Ashby, reflecting his research interests in early guidebooks to Rome, Italian topography, history and archaeology, and bound editions of engravings — including a significant collection of Piranesi prints. Enhanced through subsequent donations, the collection continues to support international research into the cultural history of Rome.
