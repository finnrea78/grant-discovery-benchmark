Investing in the future of UK research

Today, the Leverhulme Trust Board announced three new partnerships that will provide support and opportunities for underrepresented groups across the earliest stages of their research career

The Leverhulme Centenary Partnerships are funded through the Trust’s £100 million centenary commitment, a commitment made to mark its 100th anniversary in 2025 to increase support for outstanding academic talent and foster groundbreaking interdisciplinary inquiry that strengthens the UK’s world-leading research base.

Professor Anna Vignoles, Director of the Trust, said:

‘We often talk about the talent pipeline in research, and the Trust has long acknowledged the need to fund academics throughout their careers to sustain a vibrant research sector. Equally vital, however, there need to be opportunities for outstanding people from underrepresented backgrounds to enter that pipeline in the first place. That is our goal with the launch of these new partnerships.’

Recognising that meaningful and measurable impact and change require long-term, system-wide interventions, the Leverhulme Trust has invested £4 million to establish three Leverhulme Centenary Partnerships.

These partnerships enable the Trust to accelerate learning and amplify impact. They will provide direct support to over 1,100 underrepresented scholars across the sciences, humanities and social sciences. From undergraduate to master’s, and doctoral studies through to early-career, these are the critical transition points in the UK academic pipeline that the Trust aims to strengthen to increase progression into research careers.

Administered by the Royal Academy of Engineering with the Royal Society, Martingale Foundation, and the British Academy, these three partnerships cover STEM and SHAPE disciplines and address multiple forms of disadvantage, including socioeconomic background, ethnicity and disability.

Research Ready

Targeting the undergraduate-to-postgraduate transition, the Royal Academy of Engineering and the Royal Society will collaborate to offer undergraduate research placements at UK universities. Building on the existing Research Ready scheme, they plan to expand to a wider range of STEM disciplines. Partnering with the universities they will support summer placements for students from underrepresented groups, including women, those from Black or other minority ethnic backgrounds, socioeconomically disadvantaged, and those with disabilities. Each placement will last eight weeks and will receive support at Real Living Wage levels, minimising the barriers to participation. Applications will be welcomed in September.

Martingale Postgraduate Scholarships

Martingale Foundation is a leading scholarship provider, with a mission to enable talented students to overcome financial and systemic barriers so they can not only access but thrive within postgraduate study. In collaboration with Leverhulme Doctoral Scholarship programmes, Martingale Foundation will widen their reach across STEM and provide more opportunities to talented researchers to pursue PhD research. Martingale’s offer includes Master’s and PhD scholarships for students from low-income backgrounds. They take a unique approach, providing comprehensive scholar support, spanning from application through to doctoral completion, combined with financial assistance and structured academic and professional development, through the Martingale Development Programme.

Inclusive Academic Futures Programme

The British Academy builds on its sector leading work in equality, diversity and inclusion innovation with this new programme, which aims to improve the representation, inclusion and progression of underrepresented early career academics in the SHAPE disciplines, including disabled researchers and researchers from underrepresented ethnic groups. Run within their established Early Career Researcher Network, the programme will target the postdoctoral stage of a researcher’s career offering pre-application training, funding to strengthen capacity, and wrap-around career development, through the Network’s mentorship scheme.

Dr Andrew Clark, Deputy CEO, Royal Academy of Engineering, said:

‘We are thrilled to be working with the Leverhulme Trust and Royal Society to expand our Research Ready scheme to cover new multidisciplinary areas. We believe that as new areas of research emerge, they should be able to attract the very best talent, irrespective of background. Through providing opportunities for earlier experiences of research, Research Ready will encourage students from underrepresented groups to pursue careers at the frontiers of research. Moreover, hosting groups of such students will lead to important changes within institutions as to how they recruit, develop, and retain research students.’

Cassandra Hugill, Chief Executive of Martingale Foundation, commented:

‘We are delighted to be working with the Leverhulme Trust to expand our STEM postgraduate scholarships and open-up access to their world-leading Doctoral Programmes.

‘At Martingale, we’re committed to helping talented researchers overcome the financial and institutional barriers that stop too many from pursuing STEM research at postgraduate level. Scholars will benefit from these Doctoral Programmes alongside Martingale's own tailored support through our Development Programme.

‘Family income should never be a barrier to excellence. This partnership means we can support more researchers with the potential to drive real advances in their fields.’

Professor Margot Finn, Vice President for Research and Higher Education Policy at the British Academy, said:

‘We are excited to be partnering with the Leverhulme Trust on their Centenary Partnerships with this pilot programme, which will strengthen support for underrepresented early career researchers across the humanities and social sciences. The new programme will bolster the British Academy’s ongoing efforts to reduce barriers to research careers through initiatives such as our Additional Needs Funding and the partial randomisation of our Small Research Grants.

‘Building on the success of our Early Career Researcher Network, this investment will provide practical training, mentoring and tailored career development at a pivotal stage in researchers’ careers. Helping talented researchers thrive will strengthen the future of UK research by ensuring that excellence can flourish wherever it is found, especially at a time of significant pressure on the higher education sector.’

Notes

The Royal Academy of Engineering creates and leads a community of outstanding experts and innovators to engineer better lives. As a charity and a Fellowship, we deliver public benefit from excellence in engineering and technology and convene leading businesspeople, entrepreneurs, innovators and academics across engineering and technology. As a National Academy, we provide leadership for engineering and technology, and independent, expert advice to policymakers in the UK and beyond. Our work is enabled by funding from the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology, corporate and university partners, charitable trusts and foundations, and individual donors. raeng.org.uk

Martingale Foundation was founded with the belief that family income should never be a barrier to academic excellence, and partners with leading UK universities to provide fully-funded STEM masters and PhD Scholarships to talented students from low-socioeconomic backgrounds. Martingale Foundation is incubated by Purposeful Ventures, a non-profit organisation working towards a fairer society where all young people thrive. Applications will open on 12 August 2026 for maths and AI postgraduate scholarships for courses starting at partner universities in Autumn 2027. Martingale Foundation

The British Academy is the UK’s national academy for the humanities and social sciences. We mobilise these disciplines to understand the world and shape a brighter future. We invest in researchers and projects across the UK and overseas, engage the public with fresh thinking and debates, and bring together scholars, government, business and civil society to influence policy for the benefit of everyone. www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk @britishacademy.bsky.social
