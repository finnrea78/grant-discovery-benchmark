£50 million invested in the future generation

The Leverhulme Trust is thrilled to announce the winners of the 2026 Leverhulme Centenary Doctoral Scholarship grants, supporting exceptional academic talent and fostering innovative interdisciplinary inquiry

Following the Trust’s £100 million centenary commitment to UK research, this current round provides more studentships, funding to support the administration of the programme as well as some postdoctoral level opportunities for Leverhulme-funded programme graduates.

Professor Anna Vignoles, Director of the Trust said,

“Doctoral scholars are the bedrock of the academic system and these scholarships provide the opportunity for people to do something exciting at the beginning of their academic career.  Our focus on interdisciplinarity enables students who are working on related topics to come together and look at an issue from different angles.”

In 2026, ten grants of up to £5 million each were awarded to UK universities, details below:

Leverhulme Doctoral Programme on Social Connectivity in Later Life 

Professor Louise Phillips, University of Aberdeen

Rhythms of Life Leverhulme Doctoral Training Programme 

Dr Amy Ellison, Bangor University

Leverhulme Transformative Farming Doctoral Programme 

Professor Fernando Auat Cheein, Harper Adams University

Fungal Frontiers: the Leverhulme Doctoral Scholarships Programme for Fungal Science 

Dr Claire Stanley, Imperial College London

Leverhulme Doctoral Scholarships Programme for Neurodiverse Futures (LPN) 

Professor Sara Upstone, Kingston University 

Professor Deborah Riby, Durham University

Leverhulme Doctoral Programme on Food for Nature and Society 

Professor Christina Hicks, Lancaster University

Leverhulme Living with Waste Doctoral Programme: Rethinking Sustainable Consumption and Transformation 

Professor Kate L Spencer, Queen Mary University of London

Next Generation Democracy: a Leverhulme Doctoral Programme on Adolescent Citizenship 

Professor Manos Tsakiris, Royal Holloway, University of London

Knowing Animals: the Leverhulme Doctoral Programme on Interdisciplinary Pathways to Multispecies Understanding 

Dr Siobhan Abeyesinghe, Royal Veterinary College, University of London 

Dr Joah Madden, University of Exeter

Leverhulme Doctoral Programme in Responsible Hybrid Intelligence 

Dr Yashar Moshfeghi, University of Strathclyde 

Professor Simon Hanslmayr, University of Glasgow
