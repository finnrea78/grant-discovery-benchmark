Ashmolean Museum of Art & Archaeology

Bailiffgate Museum & Gallery is dedicated to preserving the heritage and culture of Alnwick and Northumberland. The Museum is housed in a former church, but the ambition, is to restore Grade I Georgian Northumberland Hall creating a new home for the museum.

Birmingham Conservation Trust

Purpose of Grant: The Golden Lion Restoration Project
Amount awarded: £20,000
Location: Birmingham
Date awarded: September 2025

The Birmingham Conservation Trust works to preserve Birmingham’s threatened architectural heritage. They are planning to conserve and bring back into use the Golden Lion Inn as a community facility.

The 16th Century, grade II timber-framed Golden Lion Inn is an extremely rare survival of a late-medieval building and a pioneering example of architectural conservation practice.  Originally built as a town house in Deritend in Birmingham city centre it was saved from demolition in 1911 by the Birmingham Archaeological Society and relocated to its current home in Canon Hill Park.

The building is at risk of destruction due to structural failure, water ingress and arson. Our grant of £20,000 will help towards repairs to make the building structurally sound, so that it can be utilised as an important community asset for local people.

Birmingham Diocesan Trust

Purpose of Grant: Stained glass window repairs
Amount awarded: £5,000
Location: Staffordshire, England
Date awarded: September 2025

Known as “Pugin’s Gem”, St Giles’ is considered to be the finest of all the churches built by the celebrated nineteenth century architect and designer A.W.N Pugin. It was completed in 1846 and is Grade I listed. Almost all the furniture and fittings were designed by Pugin, the windows were the work of William Wailes to Pugin’s instructions.

A recent expert inspection of the stained-glass windows reported most were in good condition but recommended some work was necessary. Broken or missing tracery panels need to be repaired or replaced; vulnerable glass breaks need work; some re-leading is necessary; and new support bars need to be installed.

Chatham Historic Dockyard

Purpose of Grant:  Conserving ‘Mars surrounded by an assembly of Gods’
Amount awarded: £25,000
Location: Chatham, Kent
Date awarded: September 2025

Chatham Historic Dockyardis a large independent museum and maritime heritage attraction situated in Chatham, Kent. It was a national facility for shipbuilding and repair for the Royal Dockyard for 400 years until it was decommissioned in 1984.

Our grant of £25,000 is towards the conservation of the 1701 ceiling painting, attributed to Sir Thomas Highmore, ‘Mars surrounded by an assembly of Gods’. In 1723 it was installed above the central staircase of the Grade I Commissioner’s House, the most intact naval residence in Britain. The painting has been damaged through water ingress and has layers of surface dirt, cracking and discoloured varnish. The conservation work, which will need to take place in situ, will stabilise, clean and repair the ceiling painting in time for the public to enjoy when the building reopens in the summer next year.

Liliesleaf Trust

Purpose of Grant:  Archival Display for Europe’s first Anti-Apartheid Museum
Amount awarded: £23,000  
Location: London
Date awarded: September 2025

The Liliesleaf Trust is a Black-led organisation, aiming to ‘advance public knowledge of Britain’s central role in the international movement to end apartheid in Southern Africa from the 1960s to 1994’. The Trust is developing Europe’s first Anti-Apartheid Museum in the building of the former headquarters of the African National Congress (ANC) and the centre for British anti-apartheid activities at 28 Penton Street, Islington.

The Trust is requesting funding towards the conservation, storage and display of some objects, photographs and ephemera including, three museum grade cases, a bespoke ‘memory box’ and an archivist’s specialist time. The objects for conservation and display are an adapted suitcase used by the activist group, the London recruits, correspondence between key activists (including Nelson Mandela), and photographs of leading activists.

Our grant of £23,000 will help make anti-apartheid materials accessible to the public for the first time. The objects will be displayed safely and within environmental standards.

London Borough of Hackney

Purpose of Grant: Conserving Portrait of a local icon
Amount awarded: £3,430
Location: Hackney, East London
Date awarded: September 2025

Hackney Museum is a local authority run community history museum celebrating the worldwide roots of the people who live and work in the borough.

Local icon and horticulturalist George Loddiges (1786-1846), was a second-generation German immigrant who introduced numerous exotic species
into western cultivation and built what was then the largest tropical hothouse in the world, in Hackney. He also landscaped much of Abney Park Cemetery in Stoke Newington and his influence spread internationally from Russia to Australia.

Our grant will go towards appointing an accredited expert to conserve an early 19th century painting of this local icon. Conservation work will allow the painting to be included in a new permanent exhibition at Hackney Museum in early 2027 relevant to people who live, work and learn in the borough.

National Museums Scotland

Purpose of Grant:  The Peebles Hoard Conservation
Amount awarded: £20,000 
Location: Scotland
Date awarded: September 2025

A hoard of Bronze Age artefacts unearthed by a metal detectorist near Peebles in the Scottish Borders had lain undisturbed for 3,000 years before it was discovered in 2020. Dating from between 1,000BC and 800BC, the hoard has been described as one of the most significant ever found in the country, and important for research and understanding Bronze Age life in Scotland.

The collection, which contains over 500 objects and fragments, was acquired by the National Museums Scotland in 2024, Many of the fragments will not survive without conservation. Our grant of £20,000will help to stabilise and preserve the hoard and enable it to be on public display in the future.

Tectona Trust

Purpose of Grant: Conservation and maintenance of Heritage Wooden tall Ship Tectona
Amount awarded: £5,000
Location: Plymouth
Date awarded: September 2025

The Tectona Trust helps people of all ages through the experience of sail training activities in traditional sailing craft, including their maintenance and conservation. Founded in 2009 and based around the heritage wooden tall ship Tectona, which it aims to preserve and maintain, the Trust offers  lifechanging voyages to those most in need in particular young people, people with mental health issues and those in recovery from addiction.

Tectona was built in 1928 in India of solid Teak, hence the name. It is on the National Historic Ship register. Despite a recent full refit some additional restoration work has been identified. Most of the work will be done by volunteers working with skilled shipwrights and costs will cover equipment, materials and parts, as well as volunteer costs and fees for the shipwrights.

Our grant will support the work ensuring the long term preservation of the vessel enabling the project, which has both heritage and social benefits, to continue.

Our grant of £20,000 will go towards the first phase of repairs to the external fabric of the building to help make it watertight. Once the much larger renovation work takes place, the long-term use and preservation of this historic community asset will be secured.

Ushaw Historic House, Chapels & Gardens

Purpose of Grant: Ushaw Libraries – Condition Survey
Amount awarded: £20,000
Location: County Durham
Date awarded: September 2025

Ushaw Historic House, Chapels & Gardens is an accredited museum and venue for cultural events with a series of interlocking buildings and extensive grounds in County Durham.

The National Portrait Gallery is home to one of the world’s most prestigious collections of portraits. Founded in 1856, the Gallery now holds over a million records of portraits made in Britain from the 16th–19th centuries.
