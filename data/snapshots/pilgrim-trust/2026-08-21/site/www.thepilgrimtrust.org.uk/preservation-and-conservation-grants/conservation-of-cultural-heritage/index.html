Conservation of cultural heritage

Our grants support the conservation of culturally and historically significant collections and objects. That includes works of art, books, significant ephemera, artefacts, and archives. We currently support the care and conservation of collections in small and medium museums through our funding of the Association of Independent Museums (AIM) and the conservation of manuscripts through our support of the National Manuscripts Conservation Trust (NMCT).

Grant applicants should only apply to the Pilgrim Trust if they are not eligible for AIM and NMCT’s grant schemes.

Need to know
Grant size: Generally, £1,000 to £30,000 (average approx. £15,000). 
What funding covers: Project costs including staff costs, fees, paid internships, initial exploratory work and conservation costs.
What funding does not cover: We do not fund general refurbishment works, acquisition costs, renewal of services, interpretation, exhibition or display costs. We do not fund options appraisals or feasibility studies.
Small grants: Grants for £5,000 or less are considered on a rolling basis between meetings. You can expect a response within 4 weeks once you have submitted a Stage two application.
Deadlines: You may apply at any time. Stage one applications are considered on a rolling basis. Stage two applications above £5,000 are considered quarterly at our Board meetings in March, June, September, November. Cut off dates in 2026 are 9 January, 10 April, 31 July, 2 October. See How to Apply below. 
Timeline:
 If successful, we normally expect a grantee to claim their grant within a year and complete the project/piece of work within two years.
Success rate: In 2024, 40% of Stage one applications and 72% of Stage two applications were successful. 77% of successful applicants were first time applicants to the Trust. Approx. 12 grants are awarded at each Trustee meeting.
Types of projects we support

Our grants support the preservation and conservation of culturally and historically significant collections and objects.

The conservation work should be carried out by an accredited conservator and informed by a conservation report including a condition assessment, proposed treatment and full costings.

We encourage projects to support paid internships or placements as part of the conservation work to support the development of the profession.

We encourage applicants to include a knowledge sharing element as an outcome of the project.

The conservation work should enable greater public access. 

 

Our strategic partners

Working with strategic partners increases our reach and impact:

We work with the Association of Independent Museums (AIM) to support small to medium sized museums in the care of their collections through conservation care audits and specific conservation projects. AIM members can apply via the AIM website.

We work with the National Manuscripts Conservation Trust (NMCT) to preserve important manuscript and archive collections found in non-national archives, libraries and universities. Visit the NMCT website to apply.

Who can apply

You can apply if you are:

  • A UK registered charity
  • An organisation with exempt charitable status
  • Recognised public body
  • Not eligible for the grant programmes run by AIM and NMCT
  • A Community Benefit Society*
  • A Community Interest Company (CIC) limited by guarantee*

Your work or project must:

  • Be UK based
  • Not be complete (we don’t provide retrospective grants)

Looking to conserve an object, artefact or collection in a place of worship? Go to Places of worship. *Please see FAQs for more information on eligibility criteria.

How to apply

  • Take the eligibility quiz and discover if you can apply for one of our grants.
  • You will not be able to access our application forms without checking your eligibility.
  • All applications must be made on our online grants system, Flexigrant.
  • Flexigrant has clear instructions on how to fill in the application forms.
  • View a sample Stage One form and a Stage Two form before applying.
  • Stage 1: submit key details about your project so that we can check its suitability. You’ll get a response within four weeks.
  • Stage 2: set out your application in full. You can apply at any time within 12 months after a successful Stage 1 application.
  • If you are applying for £5,000 or less, you should receive a response within 4 weeks.
  • If you are applying for over £5,000 our Trustees make final decisions at their quarterly meetings in March, June, September and November. Cut off dates in 2026 are 9 January, 10 April, 31 July, 2 October. ​

If you have any questions, email us on info@thepilgrimtrust.org.uk.
